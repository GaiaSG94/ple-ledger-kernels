An engineering agent harness in which the LLM only specifies. Typed facts are extracted with provenance, deterministic kernels compute with unit checks, and every number in the deliverable is audited dimension by dimension.

Main finding

Taking reading and arithmetic away from the model raises the dev-set score from 0.11 to 0.31F2 while tokens per task fall from 62 k to 24 kF2. The cost moves from tokens to CPU.

§1 · for readers without an engineering background

01Why reading and arithmetic break agents

An engineering task hands the agent a drawing, a specification and a question: how much does this beam bend, which cable section carries this load. On the dev set, the two heaviest failure families are not reasoning errors. They are misread drawing callouts and unit errors.

A callout like 6000 on a structural drawing means millimetres. Read as metres, the bending moment is off by a factor of a million, and the model writes it down with full confidence. Language models are good at deciding what to compute and poor at reading small print and carrying units through arithmetic. So we split the job.

6000 → mm, not m.
×10⁶ error, written calmly.

§5 · Limits & ideas that failed

05Where it still fails

  • Twelve kernels, not more. Tasks outside them take the generic sympy/pint path, which is slower and scores lower.
  • Paid code tables are out of scope. Normative values come from tables shipped with the task or are recomputed, source cited.
  • Raster drawings depend on OCR confidence; the verbatim text is always given to the model alongside the ledger.
  • Replicating public verify scripts was dropped: it reads as hard-coded output.
